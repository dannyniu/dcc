> This note eventually guides the choice
> for "mini.h" on design of stack operations.

This is a stack model where:

- All datum may have a minimum alignment requirement, or may be packed.
  Either way, this can be consolidated as the assumption that the
  minimum alignment requirement is 1 or greater.

- Stack pointer may have alignment requirement with
  a minimum of 1 or greater.

- Stack should not be over-allocated, leaving empty space above callee.

- The alignment requirement of stack and of datum may differ.

As such:

- Allocation of stack space for function calls have more constraint than the
  allocation of stack space for temporary variables.

- Allocation of function argument stack may need to re-order register-passed
  arguments and stack-passed arguments.

- When emitting function calls, the precise steps of placement of arguments
  must consult _ABI knowledge_.

- When the stack pointer must fulfill some ABI-specific alignment
  requirement(s), the mental picture of the compiler keeps track of
  the effective stack pointer (rather than the real stack pointer in
  the register file), by remembering an addend, and only increase
  stack space when the addend would flow over.

When calling function, re-ordering of arguments may happen:

- The compiler evaluates the type of its operands/arguments,
  to prepare for their placements.

  - The _info source_ for type definition include
    - the declarations associated with the declarator,
    - the type name.

  - The ABI knowledge is consulted, supplying the said info source
    to produce _ABI type description_, which include:
    - size and alignment (which are mandatory fields for all ABIs)
    - any other information that may influence the placement of arguments.

  - The ABI knowledge is also consulted to determine the placement
    of arguments for the following later steps.

- Pointer-substituted stack arguments (if any) are evaluated first,
  so that their placement don't conflict with the rest of the stack

- Arguments that're to be passed on stack are then evaluated,
  in order in accordance to the growing direction of the stack.

- The rest of the arguments are then evaluated.
